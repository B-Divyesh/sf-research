# Accessibility research — 2026-08-27

- remote-screen-reader — locked-down and visual-only software still leaves people without a usable reader.
- typo-context-checker — dyslexia-related token confusions can survive normal review and reach production.
- transcript-tidy-reader — people who prefer text still face fragmented, copy-paste-only video transcripts.
- intent-voice-macros — narrowly scoped, visible voice actions can unlock repeated tasks without a general AI agent.
- name-vibration-captions — live captions need a personal haptic cue for names and urgent phrases.
- speaker-lane-captions — mixed-speaker caption walls remain hard to follow in in-person groups.
- color-meaning-audit — global color filters do not explain whether a live chart or UI hides meaning in color alone.
- motion-comfort-card — players still need a practical way to test and retain the settings that reduce nausea.
- eink-reading-profile — compact readers need portable comfort settings, not another device-specific setup ritual.
- focus-flow-map — keyboard users and maintainers need a reproducible map of the actual focus route.
- screen-reader-smoke-test — small teams need a reviewable announcement contract for a critical flow.
- pronunciation-cue-reader — read-aloud users still need durable pronunciation fixes for their own vocabulary.
