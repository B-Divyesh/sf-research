# Accessibility opportunity research

screen-bridge — visual-only and remote interfaces still evade semantic accessibility APIs; a local, user-invoked bridge targets that gap.
page-pointer — a parent explicitly wants camera-assisted word guidance for shared paper reading, without scanning or a dedicated device.
caption-clarity — captions remain unreliable and unpersonalized for hearing loss; user-owned WebVTT can support better emphasis now.
caption-fix-queue — caption generation is automated, but a small team still lacks a fast, explainable review queue for harmful defects.
dialog-only-switch — learners repeatedly get CC sound cues when they need spoken dialogue; a reversible local view is a narrow fix.
transcript-pocket — sidecar captions exist but audio-only players still make them hard to consume comfortably and privately.
color-context — global color filters do not explain what a particular color means; local annotations and texture cues can.
calm-scroll — ordinary page motion can cause nausea, and OS reduced-motion settings do not reliably tame individual sites.
code-echo — dyslexic developers need selective code-aware reading support, not a generic screen reader or an AI coding assistant.
focus-resume-card — ADHD-friendly work needs rapid interruption recovery from a concrete next action, not another extensive task system.
eye-comfort-profiles — individual reading geometry matters; per-site, persistent comfort settings remain unnecessarily awkward.
headset-cue-check — screen-reader users need a practical, independently operable way to validate a headset and preserve a setup recipe.
