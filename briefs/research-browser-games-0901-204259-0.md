# Browser-games research — 2026-09-01

- couch-crew — Phone-controller party play has proven setup demand, but cooperative asymmetric action is still missing from mini-game portals.
- browser-bridge-crew — A current request explicitly asks for browser-controlled school play and bridge-simulation roles rather than phone controllers.
- pocket-draft-duel — One-host instant join is valued, while a short fresh draft avoids card-collection friction.
- route-of-the-day — Daily web-game players are explicitly looking beyond word games for a fresh five-minute ritual.
- tide-and-tile — Novel daily puzzles need a gentle difficulty ramp; tactile rotation play validates the short calming format.
- mirror-orchard — Players explicitly ask daily visual-puzzle makers for an archive, not one gated attempt per day.
- last-lap-breakout — Browser roguelite players enjoy perk-driven repeat rounds, but a visible eight-minute ending is underserved.
- finite-forge — Incremental fans praise clean resets while also reporting mobile friction; a finite portrait campaign is a principled gap.
- tap-trail — Browser rhythm interest currently routes through an extension and YouTube, leaving a clean original-audio web game open.
- one-screen-sprint — People actively seek reaction-heavy browser excitement; one-keyboard local play is unlike a multiplayer queue.
- practice-after-daily — Niche daily games need discoverable, ungated practice for learners rather than hiding it after completion.
- room-code-mystery — Social-browser players object to party-game pack distribution and want a focused no-account remote room experience.
