# Browser-games research — 2026-09-01

couch-creatures — Families are explicitly asking for sane, low-friction local co-op without controller or dark-pattern baggage.
pause-garden — Remote co-op players need interruption-tolerant play, particularly for short nights and care duties.
hand-of-two — Couples want compact two-player card strategy without a collectible economy or install.
aisle-47 — Travel/local groups want private, ad-free social play that works when connectivity is unreliable.
rulebook-relay — Daily-puzzle players are asking for original mechanics whose rules can change without becoming opaque.
dawn-run — Free daily web-game demand and same-seed browser roguelikes point to an approachable non-word daily run.
rankless-rally — Daily leaderboards demonstrably drive replays, while archives prevent a one-puzzle-per-day dead end.
puzzle-late — People finish a daily puzzle and want more immediately; an original mobile web anthology avoids IP-dependent archives.
gate-shift — There is appetite for compact, mechanic-led grid puzzles with an explicit finish rather than endless retention loops.
orderly-chaos — Partial-order deduction is an underused, teachable puzzle mechanic for a short visual browser game.
signal-school — Players describe learning abstract systems through shared play; a finite co-op game can serve that need without becoming an LMS.
roomcode-tactics — Browser turn-based play remains active, and resilient room-code games avoid the cost and friction of matchmaking.
