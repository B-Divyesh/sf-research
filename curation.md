# Featured picks

These 12 pages were opened live. Each has a working first screen and represents a different, finished kind of release.

- `audio-gap-loop` — A learner can turn their own clip into a listen, speak, and check loop. That makes language practice immediately personal instead of providing another generic player.
- `change-diff-inbox` — It watches selected page regions and separates meaningful changes from page churn. That is a serious improvement on screenshot monitoring.
- `claim-ready-homebook` — A private home inventory matters most after a loss. Claim exports turn it into a record that can survive the stressful moment it was made for.
- `mechanism-playground` — Building cams, gears, and linkages is both a toy and a direct way to understand motion. The interaction carries the lesson without turning into coursework.
- `open-quiz-arena` — A teacher can run a live quiz with no accounts or player cap. Removing those two practical barriers is more valuable than adding another classroom dashboard.
- `pairplay-motion` — It gives spare phones a physical role as motion controllers for short room games. That is a distinctive social hook, not just another shared-screen collection.
- `parallel-ebook-reader` — Readers can align their own DRM-free editions and export sentence pairs. It creates a specific language-learning use from books they already own.
- `quote-acceptance-packet` — It records the exact quote and the client decision. That small piece of evidence can prevent a costly dispute when a job changes shape.
- `static-qr-no-signup` — It makes static codes locally with no redirect service in the middle. Printed links need that permanence more than they need a branded QR dashboard.
- `creative-cartridge` — Six bounded activities give a child or family a clear project to start and finish. It is more convincing than an empty creative canvas with a prompt.
- `takeout-photo-metadata-fixer` — Google Photos sidecars are a real archive problem, and this directly repairs their dates and locations. It solves the part of a Takeout migration people discover too late.
- `wordlist-arcade` — One vocabulary list becomes six playable classroom games without accounts or arbitrary limits. That is a rare case where the simple version is also the useful version.

# First screens that need work

- `timeline-json-viewer` — “Private route atlas” hides that it opens exported Google Maps Timeline JSON. Lead with that job and put a sample-file action first.
- `motion-curve-playground` — “Shape the pause” is a metaphor. Say that it edits motion curves, previews frames, and exports the result.
- `dyslexia-reading-resume` — “Stop reading” opens with the interruption, not the benefit. Name the saved sentence and resume action before the supporting copy.
- `recall-calibrator` — “Setting the press” gives a cold visitor no clue about recall confidence. Name the practice cards and show the first comparison immediately.
- `mechanism-playground` — The page’s largest heading is the product name. Lead with building and testing moving mechanisms, then use the name as supporting text.
