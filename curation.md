# Featured picks

- `accessible-explanation-checkin` — Students explain an answer and state confidence before a teacher responds. That reveals misunderstanding a score alone cannot show.
- `audio-gap-loop` — It turns a learner's own clip into a private listen, speak, and check exercise. The supplied material makes practice immediately relevant.
- `change-diff-inbox` — It watches chosen page regions and shows a semantic change for review. That is more useful than screenshot monitoring that mostly produces noise.
- `claim-ready-homebook` — A private home inventory becomes valuable when something has gone wrong. Claim exports make it a durable record, not a list of possessions.
- `mechanism-playground` — Gears, cams, and linkages can be assembled and tested as a toy. The same interaction teaches mechanical relationships without becoming a lesson plan.
- `open-quiz-arena` — Live classroom quizzes work without accounts or player caps. That removes two common obstacles for a teacher in front of a real class.
- `pairplay-motion` — Spare phones become motion controllers for short room games. The physical hook distinguishes it from the catalogue's other shared-screen games.
- `parallel-ebook-reader` — Readers can align their own DRM-free editions and export sentence pairs. That is a concrete language-learning capability, not a vague reading aid.
- `quote-acceptance-packet` — It records exactly what was quoted and what a client accepted. Freelancers get evidence that still matters when a job changes shape.
- `static-qr-no-signup` — It creates static QR codes locally, with no redirect service between maker and destination. Those details matter when printed links must keep working.
- `android-select-speak-canvas` — A person can select text in an image and hear it on-device. The first action is clear and removes a real reading barrier.
- `creative-cartridge` — Six bounded activities give a child or family something to start and finish. It is a more convincing creative product than an empty canvas with a prompt.

# First screens that need work

- `timeline-json-viewer` — “Private route atlas” hides that it opens exported Google Maps Timeline JSON. Lead with that job and put a sample-file action first.
- `motion-curve-playground` — “Shape the pause” is a metaphor. Say that it edits motion curves, previews frames, and exports the result.
- `dyslexia-reading-resume` — “Stop reading” opens with the interruption, not the benefit. Name the saved sentence and the resume action before the supporting copy.
- `recall-calibrator` — “Setting the press” gives a cold visitor no clue about recall confidence. Name the practice cards and show the first comparison immediately.
- `mechanism-playground` — The page's first large heading is the product name. Lead with building and testing moving mechanisms, then use the name as supporting text.
